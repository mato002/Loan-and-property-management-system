# Property Module Marketing Guide

Practical source brief for campaign planning, product graphics, demos, and video scripts. This guide covers the **Property module only**. It describes capabilities present in the application; availability of payment channels, portals, permissions, and integrations depends on the customer’s configuration.

## 1. Product in one sentence

The Property module helps property teams manage properties and units, leasing, rent collection, maintenance, owner reporting, and the financial records that connect those activities.

## 2. Positioning and message hierarchy

**Primary promise:** Bring day-to-day property operations into one connected workspace, from a vacant unit or lease through payments, service requests, and owner reporting.

**Supporting messages:**

- See properties, units, occupancy, tenants, and leases in a shared operating view.
- Keep rent billing, payment records, allocations, receipts, arrears, and reconciliation connected.
- Give tenants and landlords access to relevant self-service information and actions through their portals.
- Track maintenance work and vendor activity from request to completion.
- Use property-focused financial reports and accounting records to understand income, costs, balances, and liabilities.

**Suggested tagline options:**

- Property operations, connected.
- From lease to ledger, in one property workspace.
- Manage the property lifecycle in one place.

Use the first as a broad campaign line. The others can support workflow-specific creative.

## 3. Who it serves

| Audience | What they need | Relevant product story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Property managers and agents | A clear view of units, leases, collections, service requests, and follow-up | Run the portfolio from a shared operations workspace |
| Landlords and property owners | Visibility into portfolio performance, earnings, expenses, statements, and payouts | Review property activity and owner reporting through the landlord workspace |
| Tenants | A convenient way to review invoices, make or record payments, access receipts, and raise maintenance requests | Handle common tenancy tasks through the tenant portal |
| Finance and collections teams | Accurate billing, allocations, receipts, arrears follow-up, reconciliations, and books | Connect rent collection activity to statements and accounting workflows |
| Maintenance coordinators and vendors | A trackable way to receive, quote, assign, and complete work | Move maintenance requests through jobs and vendor workflows |
| HR and operations staff | Employee records and leave workflows within property operations | Keep employee and leave administration alongside the operating team |

## 4. What the Property module includes

### Portfolio and properties

- Property, landlord, and unit records.
- Unit amenities and occupancy/performance views.
- Portfolio-level navigation and property reporting.

**Creative angle:** “Know what you manage.” Show a portfolio view leading into property, unit, and occupancy details.

### Listings and leasing

- Create and manage property listings, including vacant and published listing workflows.
- Track leads and applications.
- Maintain tenant and lease records, including lease creation, renewal, updates, and termination workflows.
- Support move-in, move-out, and tenant notices.

**Creative angle:** “Keep the journey from vacancy to tenancy visible.” Show a listing progressing to an application, tenant, and active lease. Do not imply third-party listing syndication unless confirmed for the specific deployment.

### Tenants and tenant service

- Tenant directory and tenant detail workspaces.
- Lease, invoice, payment, and statement information.
- Tenant credits and deposits.
- Tenant portal access to relevant tenancy, payment, receipt, invoice, notification, and maintenance-request features.

**Creative angle:** “Give tenants a clearer place to handle the essentials.” Use tenant-facing portal screens with realistic demo data.

### Revenue and collections

- Rent roll, arrears, and uninvoiced lease views.
- Invoice and billing workflows.
- Payment recording and payment allocation to invoices.
- Receipts and tenant payment history.
- M-Pesa payment workflows where configured, including STK/payment initiation options.
- Bank statement import and reconciliation workflows.
- Collections reporting, period closing, and penalty-rule configuration.
- Utility billing, utility reconciliation, and utility intelligence workflows.

**Creative angle:** “Follow the money from invoice to receipt.” Show the invoice, payment, allocation, updated tenant balance, and receipt as one story. Bank reconciliation and M-Pesa availability depend on the deployment’s configuration and supported setup.

### Maintenance and vendors

- Maintenance requests, jobs, history, costs, and frequency views.
- Vendor directory and vendor details.
- Request-for-quote/bidding and quote workflows.
- Vendor performance and work records.
- Tenant maintenance request submission and landlord-facing request/approval views where enabled.

**Creative angle:** “Make maintenance easier to track.” Show a request becoming an assigned job, vendor quote, work record, and completed item. Avoid promising response-time guarantees.

### Landlord workspace

- Landlord portfolio and property views.
- Earnings and remittance/payout information, including payout settings and withdrawal request workflows where enabled.
- Income, expense, cash-flow, and statement reports.
- Rent roll, arrears, owner statements, and vacancy views.
- Lease and maintenance information relevant to the owner.

**Creative angle:** “Owners can see the activity behind their portfolio.” Present an owner statement or cash-flow view, with sensitive identifying and financial data replaced by demo values.

### Property finance and accounting

- Income and expense tracking, cash flow, owner balances, and commissions.
- Receivables and tenant statements; payables, landlord payables/payouts, and accounts payable workflows.
- Cash book and bank reconciliation.
- Trial balance, income statement, balance sheet, aged receivables/payables, and deposit-liability reports.
- General ledger, chart of accounts, journals, payroll, audit/reversal, and period workflows.

**Creative angle:** “Connect property activity to financial visibility.” Show a rent receipt feeding the relevant property finance views. Do not claim independent audit certification or guaranteed accounting compliance.

### Communications, analytics, and administration

- SMS/email communications, bulk SMS, templates, conversations, and scheduled communications.
- Collection-rate analytics and operational/financial reports covering tenants, landlords, expenses, maintenance, and finance.
- Employee directory and leave workflows.
- User/role access, commission settings, M-Pesa configuration, and property system setup.

**Creative angle:** “Keep teams informed and work organized.” Use examples of a scheduled tenant reminder, collection report, or role-based workspace.

## 5. Role-based product stories

### Property manager: start-to-finish operating day

1. Open the portfolio and review properties, occupancy, and operational follow-ups.
2. Check vacant units, listings, leads, and applications.
3. Review tenant leases, invoices, arrears, payments, and maintenance requests.
4. Assign maintenance work and coordinate a vendor quote/job.
5. Review collection, expense, and property reports.

### Finance/collections team: invoice to reconciliation

1. Review the rent roll, uninvoiced leases, and invoices.
2. Record or receive a payment through the configured channel.
3. Allocate it to the appropriate tenant invoice and issue/access the receipt.
4. Review unmatched items and reconcile imported bank activity where applicable.
5. Check tenant balances, arrears, and accounting reports.

### Tenant: self-service essentials

1. Sign in to the tenant portal.
2. Review invoices, payment history, receipts, and relevant lease information.
3. Use an available payment option or view payment instructions.
4. Submit a maintenance request and review notifications.

### Landlord: portfolio visibility

1. Open the landlord workspace and review properties and vacancy information.
2. Check earnings, remittances, and owner statements.
3. Review income, expenses, cash flow, rent roll, and arrears.
4. Follow relevant lease or maintenance activity.

These are demonstration flows, not a promise that every account has identical permissions or configuration.

## 7. Graphics and screenshot capture plan

Capture the real product UI at a consistent browser size and zoom. Use a prepared demo account and fictional data; hide browser tabs, real tenant/landlord names, phone numbers, account references, balances, notifications, and URLs that reveal customer information.

1. **Portfolio overview:** property list or dashboard with a small set of fictional properties and clear status indicators.
2. **Unit visibility:** property detail showing units, occupancy, and amenities.
3. **Vacancy to lease:** listing, lead/application, and lease screens presented as a simple sequence.
4. **Tenant workspace:** tenant profile with lease and statement context.
5. **Collections lifecycle:** invoice → payment allocation → updated balance/receipt.
6. **Payment options:** tenant payment screen showing only channels actually enabled in the demo environment.
7. **Reconciliation:** bank statement row and its reconciliation status, using safe sample records.
8. **Maintenance lifecycle:** request → job → quote/vendor → completion/history.
9. **Landlord visibility:** owner statement, earnings, or cash-flow view.
10. **Financial reporting:** a representative report or accounting view with demo figures.
11. **Tenant portal:** invoices, receipts, payment history, or request submission.
12. **Responsive use:** repeat selected tenant/landlord views on mobile only after checking the actual mobile layout.

For graphic overlays, use short labels such as **Portfolio**, **Leasing**, **Collections**, **Maintenance**, and **Owner reporting**. Prefer a real UI screenshot with restrained annotation over invented interface elements.

## 8. Video concepts and draft scripts

### 30-second awareness video

**Visual sequence:** portfolio → lease → invoice/payment → maintenance request → owner report.

**Voiceover:** “A property business runs on connected details: units, leases, rent, service requests, and owner reporting. The Property module brings those workflows into one workspace, helping your team see what is happening and keep the next step moving. Explore property operations, from portfolio to collections and maintenance, in one connected view.”

**End card:** “Property operations, connected. Book a walkthrough.”

### 60–90-second product walkthrough

**Opening:** “Meet the Property module: a shared workspace for property operations.”

**Portfolio:** “Start with properties and units, then review occupancy, vacancies, listings, and leasing activity.”

**Tenant and collections:** “Manage tenant and lease details, create and review invoices, record payments, allocate receipts, and follow arrears. Available payment and reconciliation options depend on your setup.”

**Maintenance:** “Track a maintenance request through jobs and vendor workflows, with history and cost information available to the team.”

**Owner visibility:** “Landlords can review portfolio information, earnings, statements, and property reports through the landlord workspace.”

**Close:** “Keep the work behind your properties connected. Book a Property module walkthrough.”

### Short role-based clips

- **For landlords (20–30 sec):** property overview → earnings/statement → vacancy or maintenance view. Message: “Stay informed about your property activity.”
- **For tenants (20–30 sec):** invoice → payment history/receipt → maintenance request. Message: “Find key tenancy information and common actions in your portal.”
- **For finance teams (30–45 sec):** invoice → payment allocation → receipt → reconciliation/report. Message: “Follow collection activity through the records.”
- **For maintenance teams (20–30 sec):** request → job → vendor/quote → history. Message: “Keep maintenance work visible from request to completion.”

## 9. Demo preparation checklist

- Use fictional properties, tenants, landlords, vendors, staff, and financial values.
- Confirm which M-Pesa, bank, messaging, and portal features are enabled in the demo environment.
- Verify that the sample invoice, allocation, balance, and receipt agree before recording.
- Prepare one successful path and one exception path, such as an unmatched bank item, without exposing live customer records.
- Confirm all visible labels, dates, currency, and figures are legible at the planned video resolution.
- Capture the same scenario in desktop and mobile layouts only where both are supported and verified.
- End videos with one clear action: book a walkthrough or request a demo.

## 10. Claim guardrails

- Say **“helps teams manage/track/see”**, not that the product guarantees occupancy, collections, faster repairs, or profitability.
- Describe integrations and payment channels as **available when configured**. Do not imply universal provider availability.
- Describe eTIMS only after the specific integration is confirmed live for the deployment. The interface includes eTIMS-related workflow/readiness language; that alone is not proof of a working integration.
- Do not claim automatic matching is infallible. Payment matching and allocation should be demonstrated with verified examples and an exception/review path.
- Do not claim listing syndication, legally compliant leases, certified accounting, guaranteed data security, or regulatory compliance without separate product and legal verification.
- Do not show real personal, tenant, financial, payment, or account data in public creative.

## 11. Proof points to collect before publishing

Marketing can become more specific once the team has verified, publishable evidence for:

- Number and type of properties, units, and active users supported in representative customer use.
- Time saved on a defined workflow, measured with a documented method.
- Collection, reconciliation, maintenance, or vacancy outcomes supported by customer-approved data.
- Exact payment, SMS/email, accounting, and eTIMS integrations enabled and supported.
- Supported devices, browsers, languages, and deployment-specific portal capabilities.
- Customer permission for names, logos, testimonials, screenshots, and results.

Until evidence is approved, keep claims feature-based and demonstrable rather than numerical.

## 12. Internal product areas to verify before a campaign shoot

This brief reflects application navigation and route/workflow coverage. Product owners should confirm current release availability and exact behavior for each target customer before publishing claims, especially for payment integrations, eTIMS, tenant/landlord portal permissions, listing publication, and reconciliation automation.
